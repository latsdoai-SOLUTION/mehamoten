How it works

Three steps from opening the screen to the first big laugh

Nothing to install, nothing to prepare. Open one screen and the digital host does the work.

01

Open one screen

On a TV in the room, shared on Zoom, or on a phone. Type the names and press start.

02

The host leads

A short warm-up for the whole room. Then two people get an everyday scene, who is who, and a first line each. The host counts down and calls "Swap!".

03

They play, they laugh

An engineer from Haifa and a sales rep from Munich, stuck in an elevator together. People who usually sit in two separate groups start playing together.

Guided closing talk + session summary

Every session can end with a 10-minute talk, step by step: a moment from the game, whether it connects to how you work together, and one small thing to try. The app tells the facilitator what to say. At the end you get a short summary to send to the manager, with no scores and no names.

Built for mixed-language teams

Nobody gets judged on their English

We rewrote the game from the ground up for groups where English is a second language for almost everyone.

Everyday scenes everyone knows

A meeting that runs too long. The last milk in the office kitchen. A first day at a new job. No local references, no slang, no puns. If it needs explaining, it's not in the game.

Short, simple instructions

One idea per sentence. Each player gets a ready first line, so nobody has to invent the opening in a second language.

The body talks too

Freeze poses, mirror games, an emotion meter, a group machine. Many tasks need a face and a gesture more than words.

Listening wins, not talking fast

No points for the quickest joke. The rule is "Yes, and...": take what your partner gave you and add one thing. Careful speakers do great.

Cards from your world

Stage packs for tech, healthcare, education and sales, plus an editor for your team's own inside jokes.

Same game in Hebrew, same license

The Hebrew version is one tap away. One purchase opens both, so the Israeli team can keep playing in Hebrew after the guests fly home.

Why improv

Why improv works for teams

Improv has been part of leadership and executive programs at leading business schools and large tech companies for years, as a way to train communication, quick thinking and teamwork.

It helps people build skills every company needs: active listening, collaboration, confidence when things are unclear, and leading without a script.

"'Yes, and...' teaches people to build on each other's ideas instead of blocking them. That is how new things get made."

Google's Project Aristotle (180+ teams) found psychological safety to be the number 1 factor in team performance. Controlled studies (Felsman et al., 2020) showed that about 20 minutes of improv raised creative thinking, tolerance for uncertainty and positive mood.

FAQ

What HR usually asks

Some of our colleagues are not confident in English. Will it work?+
That is exactly who we built it for. The scenes are everyday situations, the instructions are short, and every player gets a first line ready on the screen. Many warm-up tasks need no words at all. Gestures, sounds and made-up words are part of the game. The facilitator guide even suggests saying it out loud at the start: nobody here is judged on their English.
Does it work with colleagues joining from abroad on Zoom?+
Yes. Share the screen (with sound) on Zoom, Teams or Meet, and the host leads everyone. Pair someone in the room with someone on the call for the scenes. It works best with cameras on.
Do people need improv or acting experience?+
Not at all. Mehamoten is made for people who have never been on a stage. It starts with a warm-up where everyone plays at the same time, so nobody stands alone in front of an audience, and the scenes start simple.
How long is a session?+
Up to you. A short 15 to 20 minute icebreaker at the start of an offsite, or a full evening of an hour or more including the closing talk.
How do we buy, and in which currency?+
The buyer is the Israeli company. Press "Buy", pay on the Israeli payment page in shekels (ILS, VAT included), and you get a ready link that opens the game for your company. Anyone can try two rounds for free first.
Is there a Hebrew version too?+
Yes, and one purchase opens both. Use English when the guests are here, and Hebrew for the local team the rest of the year.
What about privacy and data security?+
The app collects no personally identifying information. Names, scores and settings are saved only on the device and are never sent to a server.
